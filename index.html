About

I work on language-model agents that use tools, and on making them small enough to train and run cheaply.

My current research asks whether the benefits of tool-token agents, which select a tool from tens of thousands in a single decoding step, can be kept without training one large model to run the entire agent loop. The answer, so far, is yes: splitting the loop where the capabilities differ lets lightweight models rival much larger agents.

I work with Prof. Wenzhuo Zhou at UC Irvine. Before that, I studied software engineering at Dalian University of Technology. I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.

Research

Under review at ACL Rolling Review, October 2026

ToolSIC: Small-Backbone Tool-Token Agents Rivaling Large Models

Tiancong Li, Wenzhuo Zhou

one shared transcript, one module active at a time

ToolSIC splits the agent loop at the tool-token interface into three lightweight modules. Click a module to see what it does.

  • The problem. Tool-token agents pick a tool from tens of thousands of APIs in one decoding step, but today they require training one large model to run the entire agent loop.
  • The idea. Split the loop at the tool-token interface into three lightweight modules, each trained only as far as its job requires: tool selection never enters trajectory training, and answers come from an untrained off-the-shelf model.
  • The result. On StableToolBench with 47K tools, 0.6B modules outperform the released ToolGen-8B by 10.9 points of grounded success, with 6× less training compute and 11× less inference compute.

The paper is under anonymous review. I am happy to share the PDF by email.
